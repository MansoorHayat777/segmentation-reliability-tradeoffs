\section{Conclusion}

Across eight six-model panels in general scenes, street scenes, and abdominal
CT, seven declared segmentation criteria disagree in a way no scalar score can
resolve: every ordering of the evaluated models contradicts at least
\NBAllMin{}--\NBAllMax{} of the non-tied preferences, and exhaustive search shows
this bound is essentially exact. Scoring each declared endpoint with its own
frozen formula improved every non-trivial endpoint on every panel, including
recent architectures and two domains never used for selection. A single
failure score did not transfer from general scenes to domains whose failures
are dominated by severe misses, and selecting it on data with matching failure
composition restored it.

The practical implication is procedural rather than a new default metric.
Evaluations should name their decision endpoint, state the composition of the
failures they target, select any failure score on data whose failures resemble
the target domain, scale component-eligibility rules with image resolution,
and report criterion conflict rather than hide it inside a single aggregate.
