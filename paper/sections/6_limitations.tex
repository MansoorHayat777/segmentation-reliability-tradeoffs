\section{Limitations}

\paragraph{Shared construction.} Policy utility, severe-miss labels, and the
component scores are all built from component-coverage measurements. Agreement
between an endpoint-specific score and its endpoint is therefore partly
expected; the conflict bound and the cross-domain behaviour of a single failure
score do not rely on this alignment. The human study (Sec.~\ref{sec:human})
provides one external check, but with four reviewers, one question, and a
moderate effect; no downstream decision outcome was evaluated.

\paragraph{Analysis order.} Formulas were selected on COCO and VOC and frozen
before every transfer evaluation. The failure-composition analysis and the
domain-matched selection were carried out after the street-scene and CT
results had been observed. The domain-matched score was selected on Cityscapes
training data only, but Cityscapes validation shares its source distribution;
KITTI and CT provide the more independent test.

\paragraph{Panels.} Each panel contains six models, and intervals are
conditional on them. The three ADE20K panels share images, and the two
street-scene panels share one Cityscapes-trained checkpoint family, so KITTI
tests domain shift for that family rather than architecture diversity. The CT
models differ widely in quality (per-model severe-miss rates
\NAmosSevRateMin\%--\NAmosSevRateMax\%), and part of one model family's training
data is not publicly documented.

\paragraph{Endpoint definitions.} Severity maps, including the clinically
motivated CT map, are declared operationalizations, not validated harm weights.
The fixed pixel threshold for component eligibility is resolution-dependent and
can saturate severe-miss labels on high-resolution street imagery; eligibility
rules should scale with image size.

\paragraph{Use in model selection.} Endpoint-specific scoring requires the user
to declare the decision objective; a misspecified objective is evaluated
faithfully but wrongly. Conventional segmentation metrics, calibration, and
cost should be reported alongside any endpoint-specific score.
